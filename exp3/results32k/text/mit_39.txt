Let $v_{run}$ be the speed of Dana running.
Let $v_{walk}$ be the speed of Dana walking.
Let $v_{skip}$ be the speed of Dana skipping.

From the problem statement:
*   "Dana can run at a rate of speed four times faster than she can walk":
    $$v_{run} = 4 \times v_{walk}$$
*   "she can skip at a rate of speed that is half as fast as she can run":
    $$v_{skip} = \frac{1}{2} v_{run}$$

We are given that she can skip at 3 miles per hour:
$$v_{skip} = 3$$

Now we can find the relationship between the speeds.
Substitute $v_{skip} = 3$ into the equation for $v_{skip}$:
$$3 = \frac{1}{2} v_{run}$$
$$v_{run} = 2 \times 3 = 6 \text{ mph}$$

Using this, we can find $v_{walk}$:
Since $v_{run} = 4 v_{walk}$:
$$6 = 4 v_{walk}$$
$$v_{walk} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5 \text{ mph}$$

So, her speeds are:
*   Running: 6 mph
*   Walking: 1.5 mph
*   Skipping: 3 mph

**2. Analyze the time distribution:**
The problem states:
*   She spends one-third of the time running.
*   She spends two-thirds of the time walking.

Total time = 6 hours.
Time running = $\frac{1}{3} \times 6 = 2$ hours.
Time walking = $\frac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4$ hours.

**3. Calculate the total distance:**
To find the total distance, we multiply the speed by the time spent traveling that specific mode.

*   **Distance running:**
    $$\text{Distance}_{run} = v_{run} \times \text{Time}_{run}$$
    $$\text{Distance}_{run} = 6 \text{ mph} \times 2 \text{ hours} = 12 \text{ miles}$$

*   **Distance walking:**
    $$\text{Distance}_{walk} = v_{walk} \times \text{Time}_{walk}$$
    $$\text{Distance}_{walk} = 1.5 \text{ mph} \times 4 \text{ hours} = 6 \text{ miles}$$

*   **Total distance:**
    $$\text{Total Distance} = \text{Distance}_{run} + \text{Distance}_{walk}$$
    $$\text{Total Distance} = 12 \text{ miles} + 6 \text{ miles} = 18 \text{ miles}$$
